Each lesson answers one question with scenes you actually watch and controls you actually use. 15–25 minutes each — or linger as long as you like.

    What makes it work

    Not a textbook. Not a video. A live scene the lesson narrates — and lets you drive.

    Scenes, not slides

    Every figure is the Solar Voyager engine. Earth really spins, planets really follow their real orbits, and the Moon’s shadow lands where it actually did on 8 April 2024.

    Captions you can rewind

    Short, kind narration with a timeline, chapters and a replay button. Turn on read-aloud if you’d rather listen; turn off captions if you’d rather just watch.

    Time, your way

    A whole year in a minute, or a month per second — then slow back down to watch an eclipse at real speed. The clock lives in every scene.

    Your turn

    Each scene ends with a tiny challenge — find the ring of fire, catch Jupiter overtaking Saturn, make it winter in Sydney. Finish them for stars.

    What you’ll be learning (if you’re a grown-up)

    Standards-aligned where it matters, kind to curious kids where it counts. Scenes work on a phone, a tablet, a laptop and an interactive whiteboard.
