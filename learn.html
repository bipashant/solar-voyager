Three courses, one real sky

Each lesson answers one question, with scenes you watch and controls you get to use. They take 15 to 25 minutes, or longer if you get distracted flying around (most people do). Start anywhere you like.

What makes it work

Somewhere between a textbook and a video: a live scene that the lesson talks you through, and then hands over.

Scenes, not slides

Every figure is the Solar Voyager engine. Earth really spins, planets really follow their real orbits, and the Moon’s shadow lands where it actually did on 8 April 2024.

Captions you can rewind

Short, friendly narration with a timeline, chapters and a replay button. Turn on Listen if you’d rather hear it, or turn the captions off and just watch.

Time, your way

A year in twelve seconds, Saturn’s 29-year orbit in half a minute, then back down to ten minutes a second to watch a shadow cross Jupiter. Every scene has a clock.

Your turn

Each scene ends with a small challenge: find the ring of fire, make it winter in Sydney, fly to Andromeda. Finish them for stars.

What you’ll be learning (if you’re a grown-up)

Each lesson maps to a big idea in the NGSS and the English national curriculum. Scenes run on phones, tablets, laptops and classroom whiteboards.
